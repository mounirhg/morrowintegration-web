Who does your paperwork today, and with what?

It's the first question I ask every business, because the answer says more than any org chart. If it's one person, by hand, every day, this audit is for you. Here is why most automation projects stall before they get there.

  • The tool came before the problem Someone buys a platform first, then goes looking for something to use it on. The pilot works, the demo looks great, and nothing in the company changes.
  • Nobody drew the process You can't automate what nobody has written down. Most of the waste sits in the steps between one program and the next, and that's the part no vendor looks at.
  • The advice came from whoever sells the tool When the same company that audits you also resells the software, the audit already knows its answer before it starts.

Four areas, and the same question in all four.

Which part of this is one person doing by hand, every day, because there was never time to fix it? And what's already automated, and what does it cost you?

M

Marketing

The flow, not the creative. How a lead comes in, where it comes from, what happens in the hours after, and how many are lost in that gap.

What usually shows up: leads with no follow-up, inconsistent messages, no way to trace where a customer came from.

S

Sales

Conversion and control. What happens when a lead comes in, who contacts them, whether there's a pipeline and a follow-up.

What usually shows up: quotes that take days to go out, deals lost in silence, everything depending on one person's memory. If sales is broken, nothing else matters yet.

D

Delivery and fulfillment

How the service gets delivered: who does what, what's manual, where the delays are, and how many times the same thing gets typed between the job closing and the job being paid.

What usually shows up: undocumented processes, bottlenecks, the same errors repeated. This is where the big opportunities usually live.

A

Administration

Where the information lives, who has access to it, how the business is controlled and how it's reported.

What usually shows up: scattered data, duplicated records, invoices that go out late, and nobody with a real picture of what's in and what's owed. The most forgotten area, and the critical one.

First fix the process, then automate it. Automating a broken process only makes it worse, and a good share of what I find needs no technology at all.

The questions people actually ask.

Do you implement what you recommend?

Not inside the audit, and that's the point. If afterwards you want me to build the first step, that's the implementation, quoted separately, and you decide with the document already in hand. You're equally free to take it to your own team or to another firm: it's written to work in all three cases.

Are you going to tell me to replace the software I already use?

No. The plan is built on top of what you have. If a tool genuinely can't do what you need, I say so with the reason in writing, but the default is always to integrate, never to replace.

When am I too small for this?

The work is built for businesses of 3 to 20 people. Below that, there's usually no hand-off between areas to map, and the audit would cost more than the problem it finds. I tell you on the first call, not after the invoice.
